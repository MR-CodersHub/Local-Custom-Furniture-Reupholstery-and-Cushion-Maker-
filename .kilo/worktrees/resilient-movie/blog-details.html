The Ultimate Guide to Choosing Your Upholstery Fabric

E
Elena Mercer
Creative Director & Fabric Specialist

Choosing the right fabric for your upholstery project is one of the most exciting — and most daunting — decisions you'll make. With over 500 options in our collection alone, it's easy to feel overwhelmed. But in my fifteen years as Creative Director here at Craftwork Atelier, I've developed a simple framework that makes the decision clear, confident and genuinely enjoyable.

Start with Durability, Not Beauty

I know this sounds counterintuitive, but the very first question isn't "what do I love?" — it's "how will this piece be used?" A statement armchair in a reading room that gets used twice a week has completely different requirements from a family sofa that endures children, pets, and daily life.

The industry measures fabric durability through the "Martindale Rub Test" — the higher the number, the more durable the fabric. Here's a quick guide:

  • Light Domestic Use: 10,000–20,000 rubs (occasional chairs, bedroom pieces)
  • General Domestic: 20,000–30,000 rubs (living room sofas, dining chairs)
  • Heavy Domestic / Commercial: 30,000–50,000+ rubs (family sofas, contract furniture)
"The most beautiful fabric in the world is wasted on a sofa it can't survive. Always start with suitability — the aesthetic choices become much easier once you know your parameters."
— Elena Mercer, Creative Director

The Aesthetic Decision: Colour, Pattern & Texture

Once you have your durability threshold, the creative conversation can begin. This is the part I love most. At this stage, I always ask three questions:

  • Is this piece meant to anchor the room or complement it?
  • Does the room have natural light, and how does that change through the day?
  • How do you want to feel when you sit on it?

Velvet creates warmth and richness — it absorbs light rather than reflecting it, making spaces feel more intimate. Linen is crisp and fresh, ideal for rooms with plenty of natural daylight. Leather develops a patina over time and is incredibly practical, especially with pets. Wool tweeds add texture and character, particularly beautiful in traditional interiors.

The Colour Rule I Live By

When in doubt, go one shade bolder than you initially planned. Fabric in a roll often appears lighter and more muted than it will look on a finished piece. The fullness and depth of an upholstered sofa will always make the colour feel richer. So if you're drawn to a mid-terracotta, commit to it. If you're eyeing a pale sage, consider moving to the next shade down on the swatch card.

Pattern Matching: More Important Than You Think

Patterned fabrics are glorious — but they require careful planning. A striped fabric or bold floral will need additional yardage to ensure the pattern aligns correctly across seams, cushion fronts and backs. This is a craft skill our artisans specialise in, but it's worth knowing that a patterned fabric can require 20–30% more yardage than a plain equivalent.

For your first reupholstery project, I often recommend a plain or subtle texture as a starting point. You can always introduce pattern on scatter cushions, which are far easier to work with and much more affordable to experiment with.

My Fabric Recommendations by Piece

  • Family Sofa: Performance velvet (30,000+ rubs), chunky linen or a wool blend. All three clean well and age beautifully.
  • Statement Armchair: Go bold. Patterned velvet, a rich jacquard or an aniline leather will make it the room's focal point.
  • Dining Chairs: A wipeable performance fabric is your best friend here. Modern performance weaves can look identical to luxury fabrics whilst being completely food and drink resistant.
  • Headboard: This is where you can indulge. Headboards take almost no wear, so you can choose purely for beauty. A button-tufted silk-blend jacquard or a deep pile velvet creates incredible drama.
  • Outdoor Cushions: Sunbrella or equivalent performance fabric only. Nothing else will survive our climate.
